# Where Precision Belongs

Part of [Computational Drafting](README.md) · v1.5.0 · 2026-10-24, Korea Standard Time (KST)

Files 00 to 03 govern the instruction, and [04-proof-carrying-analysis.md](04-proof-carrying-analysis.md) governs what comes back. This file takes up a question put to the discipline from outside: whether the authoritative form of a rule should be a precise, machine-readable language, with natural language demoted to a drafting aid.

The authorizing party, a term used throughout this file, is the principal or, where the specification answers to a rule issued by someone else, that rule's issuer or the person with authority to interpret it. The translator is whoever, person or model, converts the prose into the precise form.

The answer has three parts. Precision belongs in the artifact read by an interpreter that must not be allowed a second reading; in most deployments, that interpreter is the runtime. No form confers authority, which comes from the institution that issues the rule; accountability lands on the human the specification names. And the precise form has to be tested against a reference that its translator did not write and that the authorizing party approved. Where that party is outside the organization and not a party to the agreement, a reading adopted for the principal (drafting consequence 3, below) serves instead. In most systems prose holds that role, because it is the text the authorizing party issued or approved and the counterparty accepted.

## The proposal

Stephen Wolfram set out the case in 2016, in "Computational Law, Symbolic Discourse and the AI Constitution." Its center is a symbolic discourse language that gives each concept one precise meaning. In the essay's words, the aim is for the symbolic form to be "the real representation: the natural language is just a guide for trying to generate it" (Code versus Language). The essay does not say which form would bind the parties; that is the question this file takes up. Three further claims bear on this repository:

- A contract combines three kinds of statement (declarative, interrogative and imperative) and so can be compared to a program (The Discourse Workflow).
- Some contract inputs are native to computation. Others must be measured in the world, and machine-learning systems may do the measuring without offering an explanation a human can follow (Where Do the Inputs Come From?).
- Converting an existing prose contract into the symbolic form yields competing readings that a person must settle; contracts in symbolic form can be simulated across many inputs to see what they produce; and lawyers will in time write in the symbolic form themselves (Writing Computational Contracts).

The discipline was built, and published as v1.0.0 on 15 July 2026, before the repository's author read the essay. This file records where the two converge and where they divide, as independent work that reached some of the same ground.

## Where the two converge

1. **Natural language underdetermines behavior.** The essay starts from the premise that natural language cannot be made fully determinate (Leibniz's Dream). The interpreter map reaches the same place from the other side: whatever the drafter leaves open is decided by the interpreter that meets it ([02-interpreter-map.md](02-interpreter-map.md)).
2. **A contract resembles a program.** The three kinds of statement loosely correspond to four of the eight elements: declarative statements, which supply facts, to Evidence (element six), interrogative statements to the conditions in Escalation (element seven) and imperative statements to Authority and Constraints (elements two and three) ([01-specification-anatomy.md](01-specification-anatomy.md)). They classify what a statement does; the anatomy also allocates who may make it, vary it or prove it was kept, and that allocation is where the two begin to divide.
3. **Testing cannot exhaust the cases.** The essay derives this from computational irreducibility and expects bugs that cannot be ruled out in advance (The World with Computational Contracts). The hostile-case checklist works within the same limit: it does not claim that a specification that survives the pass is safe, only that the failures somebody imagined have been drafted against ([03-hostile-case-checklist.md](03-hostile-case-checklist.md)).

## Where the two divide: two sources of truth

"Source of truth" carries two meanings that the proposal joins. One is the source of truth for what should happen: the obligation, read by the principal, the counterparty, the auditor and the court. The other is the source of truth for what does happen: the control, read and executed by the runtime. The [clause-to-control map](templates/clause-to-control-map.md) keeps them in separate columns and records, row by row, when someone last confirmed that they say the same thing.

Both checks the essay proposes, simulation across many inputs (Writing Computational Contracts) and formal analysis (Contracts into Code), need a statement of what the result should be. Whoever writes that statement holds the reference. If the precise form is the only statement of the rule, the expected results come from the translator's own reading. The essay expects that lawyers will in time draft in the symbolic form themselves, and Catala, a programming language for legislative text (Merigoux, Chataing and Protzenko, 2021), is designed so that lawyers can review and certify the code that programmers write. Both narrow the gap between the readers of prose and the readers of code.

A principal able to read the code still needs expected results from someone other than the person who wrote it. A test suite also covers only the cases someone listed; a text with a stated purpose reaches the rest, which is why the anatomy makes the objective the tiebreaker when operative language runs out (element one).

The essay is right that prose is imprecise; prose holds the reference role for the institutional reason stated at the start of this file. Another reference can serve, such as a set of test cases or the examples Wolfram's 2023 essay (discussed below) proposes for terms that resist definition, provided the authorizing party approved it (or the principal adopted it, as above), the translator did not write it and a stated objective governs the cases it does not list (element one).

Wolfram's position that one precise artifact carries the rule fits the runtime in most deployments. The runtime asks whether an action is technically permitted for this identity now, and that question needs an artifact that admits one reading: a permission set, a scope, a gate, a limit (elements two, three and seven). For the other interpreters, the property in the interpreter map holds: each reads a different artifact, and the drafter keeps the translations consistent.

The working formal languages bear this out. Catala keeps the legislative text in the file and pairs each provision with the code that implements it. LegalRuleML can link each rule to the source provisions it renders (section 4.3.1), record mutually exclusive alternative renderings of the same norm (sections 3.4 and 4.2.4) and state which rule overrides which (sections 3.4, 3.7 and 4.2.1). It can also name who stands behind a norm as its authority (sections 3.4 and 4.3.4); naming an authority in a format does not confer it. Harry Surden's account of data-oriented contracting marks the boundary case: obligations represented as data rather than, or as well as, prose, as with equity options, where the data form carries the obligation itself. That case is available here only by the express election described in drafting consequence 2.

A precise form does not by itself make anyone accountable. A formal rule can be wrong, and the human the specification names answers for the error, whoever drafted the rule ([README](README.md#design-principles), design principle 5).

## What language models changed

**Fact.** The essay treated any instruction to an artificial intelligence (AI) system as incomplete and proposed, if the symbolic language proved complete enough, a code of conduct in symbolic form (What Does It Mean for AIs?). It treated natural language as an entry point that a system converts into the precise form (Code versus Language), and it gave machine learning the job of measuring facts (Where Do the Inputs Come From?). Language models now execute instructions written in natural language with no precise form in between. Wolfram's 2023 essay still proposes, with a hedge, that a constitution for AI systems be drafted in a formal computational language such as his own, with its imprecise terms defined by example and generalized by neural networks (Governance in an AI World).

**Inference.** The essay's case for a symbolic layer rested on precision. Language models strengthen that case at the runtime, because imprecise text is now executed directly by an interpreter that fills its gaps at speed and does not stop to ask ([02-interpreter-map.md](02-interpreter-map.md)). They also remove the pressure to build that layer, because the system runs whether or not it exists. This repository's division is the response: the model reads natural language drafted for a probabilistic reader, the runtime reads permissions that admit one reading and the clause-to-control map keeps the two consistent.

**Uncertainty.** Whether model interpreters will become reliable enough to carry a constraint without a runtime counterpart is not known. Until they are shown to be, the rule of the [system-prompt template](templates/system-prompt-as-delegated-authority.md) holds: a prompt-only constraint is a preference.

## Drafting consequences

None of the following adds an element, a pattern or a checklist question, and no instrument file changes. Consequences 1 to 3 add rules for using the clause-to-control map, the Precedence element and the claim-evidence matrix, and consequence 2 is backed by a drafting brief for a text-and-code clause; the v1.5.0 changelog entry records the rules and the brief. Consequences 4 and 5 apply existing elements to questions the essay raises.

1. **Keep the obligation and the control as independently authored forms, and test their agreement.** They may sit in one file, as in Catala, or in several; the anatomy does not require a single document. In each row of the clause-to-control map, the obligation column names the text that states what should happen and the technical expression column names the control that makes it happen. A control that answers to no obligation has no authorization the map can trace. Where an obligation has no control, the row may stand as organizational only if its "None" is written down and accepted.
2. **Draft the conflict rule, and route detection.** State in Precedence (element four) which form governs the parties' rights when the obligation and the control diverge; the default this repository recommends is the text. That clause allocates consequences after the event; detection and suspension are what stop execution. Detection runs through automated conformance tests against expected results specified by the authorizing party (in the worked translation below, the issuer of the policy); acceptance sampling (element eight); re-verification of the map row whenever either form changes; and the evidence record (element six). On detection, the affected function moves at once to a state named in advance, closed by default for irreversible actions, until a named human releases it.

   An election that the code governs suits terms computable on their face, and it needs code pinned by version and hash, a counterparty with access to that code and a provision signed by both parties, in the agreement or by amendment, naming the requirement and a version that has passed the authorizing party's tests. Where the text and the code sit in an agreement, a clause has to settle the eleven points in the brief below.

3. **Record a translation into precise form as an interpretation.** Whether a person or a model converts prose into code, the result asserts a reading the prose does not state on its face. Record the reading as an Interpretation claim in the [claim-evidence matrix](templates/claim-evidence-matrix.md), resting on the Rule row for the source text, with the competing readings and why the translator did not choose each. Record the conformance of the code to that reading as a separate Inference. Make a person with authority to interpret the source the owner of the Interpretation row, and carry it into Section 3 of the matrix as an unresolved item until that person adopts the reading. Where that person is outside the organization and not a party to any agreement under which the code runs, as with a regulator or a court, the principal names an owner with authority to adopt the reading for the principal's purposes, and the row stays in Section 3 as a residual risk that the issuer reads the rule otherwise. The matrix then records which reading every test assumed and who adopted it.
4. **Draft deliberate open texture as a delegation.** Drafters leave a term such as *reasonable* open by design, as a reader's comment on the essay notes (Sander, 2016). Authority names the interpreter who resolves it (element two). Escalation names an observable trigger for the term, such as a listed subject matter, field or value, and stops the machine when the trigger fires, so that a model does not settle by inference what the parties left for a person to settle (element seven).
5. **Treat an oracle as an interpreter that finds facts.** A grading model, a sensor feed or an external data service that decides whether a condition is met decides facts the action turns on. Enumerate it as a source (element two), preserve its version, input and output with the decision (element six) and stop on disagreement among redundant sources or on an input unlike those it was validated on (element seven). An oracle that is gamed is Pattern 1; one adopted because it is cheaper than inspection is Pattern 4.

## Brief for a text-and-code clause

This brief sets out what a clause governing a requirement expressed in both text and code has to settle, with the default this repository recommends for each. The brief is for counsel to turn into operative wording, and the commercial points it leaves open are marked as such. Four roles run through it.

- **Principal:** the party on whose behalf the specification is drafted, whose named human answers for it.
- **Authorizing party:** as defined at the start of this file. Where the authorizing party is outside the principal's organization and not a party to the agreement, the principal performs its functions, and the expected results the principal specifies are a reading of the rule, recorded under consequence 3.
- **Reviewing party:** the party to the agreement other than the implementer.
- **Implementer:** the party that writes and deploys the code. Where the principal is also the implementer, there are two routes. The expected results can come from a person in the principal's organization independent of the implementation, who performs the authorizing party's functions (points 4 to 7, 10 and 11), the bar in point 4 applying to the personnel who wrote or deployed the code, as in the internal-policy paragraph below. Or the parties give the reviewing party all of the authorizing party's functions (points 4 to 7, 10 and 11), and the principal adopts that party's expected results in advance as its approval.

The eleven points, each with its recommended default:

1. **What the code is.** Every deployed version, each identified by version number and hash in a schedule before it is deployed.
2. **What the text is, and when they diverge.** The provisions other than the schedules listing the code and the tests; a divergence is any result the code produces, or fails to produce, that is inconsistent with the text.
3. **Which prevails.** The text, to the extent of the inconsistency, except for a requirement elected under point 10.
4. **Who writes the tests.** The authorizing party specifies test inputs and expected results; the implementer, or anyone who wrote the code, may propose inputs, which count only when adopted, and may not propose expected results; initial tests exist before first deployment, and each detected divergence gets its own test.
5. **The deployment gate.**
   - (a) Except under (b), no version deploys unless it passes the tests.
   - (b) A version failing a test whose expected result has been referred under point 7 may deploy only with the affected function held under point 6.
   - (c) The authorizing party updates the tests within a fixed period after an amendment to the text; if it does not, the implementer is not in breach for failing to conform the code to the amended text until they are updated, and point 9 does not reach executions in that period.
6. **Detection and suspension.**
   - (a) Each party notifies the other promptly on becoming aware of a possible divergence.
   - (b) The implementer runs the tests on every change and at a fixed interval, delivers the results and retains for a fixed period a record of each execution binding input, output and code version, available under point 11.
   - (c) On notice under this point or a failed test, the implementer moves the affected function at once to the state named for it in the code schedule, by default one in which it executes no irreversible action.
   - (d) A named person for the authorizing party releases the function within a fixed period after any of: (i) a correction that passes the tests, including the test added for that divergence under point 4; (ii) an amendment whose updated tests the code passes; or (iii) a finding of no divergence by that person or, on a reference, by the expert under point 7.
   - (e) If that person neither releases it nor refers the matter under point 7 within that period, that person is deemed to have released the function at the end of the period.
   - (f) Who bears the cost of a suspension that proves unfounded is commercial.
7. **Disputes.** Either party may refer, and the implementer must refer within a fixed period if it disputes a divergence; a named expert decides whether a divergence exists and whether an expected result conforms to the text, and sets the conforming result where it does not; the authorizing party records that result and has adopted it in advance as its approval.
8. **Cure or amendment.** Unless the parties amend the text instead, the implementer conforms the code within a fixed period running from the failed test or notice or, where the divergence is disputed, from the determination; a test not yet updated after an amendment starts no period. The amendment states whether it reaches back to transactions already executed. Failure to cure within the period, or to refer a disputed divergence under point 7, is a material breach.
9. **Remedy.** Correction of each affected transaction from the first divergent execution, or compensation where it cannot be reversed; none for a divergence caused by an expected result found not to conform; whether the remedy sits outside a liability cap is commercial.
10. **Election that the code governs.**
   - (a) Only by a provision signed by both parties, in the agreement or by amendment, naming the requirement and a version by hash that has passed the authorizing party's tests under point 4.
   - (b) The provision locks that version for that requirement until a further signed provision.
   - (c) For that requirement the code's result is the parties' entitlement, and points 5, 6(a), 6(c) to 6(f) and 7 to 9 do not apply.
   - (d) An error in the locked version is corrected only by a further signed provision naming a version that has passed the tests under point 4.
11. **Access and schedules.** The implementer maintains the code schedule and the authorizing party the test schedule, each updated by notice without formal amendment; a suspension state changes only with the authorizing party's written consent; read-only access to the code, its history and the execution records for the reviewing party and its auditors, under confidentiality.

In an internal policy, the system owner stands in the implementer's place and the policy owner in the authorizing party's, provided the policy owner is the policy's issuer or the person with authority to interpret it; points 8 and 9 then need internal remedies in place of breach and compensation, a named superior of the policy's issuer takes the expert's place in point 7 and the policy owner takes the place of the reviewing party in point 11.

The Controller notifies the CFO and moves the function to the hold state named in advance for map row 1, under which every payment call routes to the Controller approval queue whatever its amount. The CFO releases it under point 6(d) once a correction passes the tests updated for the CFO's rulings on rows 3 and 4 or, if the code passes them unchanged, on the CFO's finding of no divergence. Moving to the hold state is a configuration change: the Verified cell of map row 1 blanks, and row 2, which describes the pre-hold version identified in the code schedule (point 1), returns to Unverified until that version is re-exported in a test environment. The tests run against that version, and it is the version the CFO releases.

Specifying that both USD 7,500 calls go through without routing accepts the split-payment exposure knowingly. A conformance test in another currency is added once the CFO has ruled on row 4; the probe call resolves row 5 as a fact about the gate and releases nothing.

## The test

For each requirement, ask which interpreter must not be allowed a second reading. Give that interpreter the precise form. Give every other interpreter the form it can read, act on and answer for. Test the precise form against a reference that the authorizing party approved and the translator did not write, before deployment and on every change. Where the authorizing party is outside the organization and not a party to the agreement, the reference is a reading adopted for the principal under consequence 3, with the residual risk recorded. State which form governs when they diverge and name the human who decides.
